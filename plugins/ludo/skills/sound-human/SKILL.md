# Humanize

AI writing has tells. Readers spot them in one sentence and stop trusting the rest. This skill removes them and keeps the meaning, the facts and the person's voice.

## If the user gave a sample of their own writing

Their sample wins over every rule below. Match its sentence length, its level of formality and its punctuation, including dashes if they use them.

## What to fix

**Punctuation**
- No long dashes (— or –). Use a period, a comma, a colon or parentheses.
- No semicolons in casual writing.

**Inflated words**
- Plain verbs: "is", "has", "uses". Not "serves as", "boasts", "leverages", "utilizes".
- Cut "delve", "tapestry", "landscape", "realm", "testament to", "plays a crucial role", "in today's fast-paced world", "it's worth noting".
- No sales language: "seamless", "cutting-edge", "game-changing", "unlock", "elevate", "empower".

**Fake structure**
- No "It's not just X, it's Y."
- No groups of three when two things or four things are true.
- No bold labels at the start of every bullet in normal prose.
- No title case in headings. Sentence case.
- No emoji unless the person uses them.

**Filler**
- No openers like "Great question!", "Certainly!", "I hope this email finds you well" (unless the person asked for formal).
- No announcing the next point: "Let's dive in", "Here's the thing".
- No closing summary that repeats the paragraph, and no upbeat sign-off line ("Exciting times ahead!").

**Vague claims**
- No "experts say", "studies show", "many believe" without a source.
- No invented facts. If a sentence needs a number, name or date that is not in the original, leave it out or ask for it.

## Method

1. Read the whole piece once for meaning. Note every fact, name, number and request. These must survive.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence.
3. Read it out loud in your head. Anything a person would not say to a friend or a professor gets rewritten again.
4. Check the facts list: every item still there, nothing added.
5. Return the new version only, unless the user asks to see what changed.

## Length

Usually shorter than the original. A cold email over 150 words is almost always too long.
