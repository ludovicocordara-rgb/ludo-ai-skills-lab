# Idea check

Most ideas die because nobody had the problem badly enough, not because the building was hard. This skill finds that out in an afternoon.

## Step 1, write the idea down in one line

"For <who>, who <problem>, <idea> lets them <outcome>, unlike <what they do today>." If the user can't fill a blank, that blank is the first thing to figure out.

## Step 2, research (use /ludo:research)

- **The problem:** where do people complain about it? Reddit, reviews of existing tools, forums, app store reviews. Collect real quotes with links.
- **Today's answer:** what do people do now instead (a spreadsheet, a group chat, a paid tool, nothing)? Anything they already pay for is the real competition.
- **Existing players:** list similar products, what they charge, and what their users complain about.
- **Size, roughly:** how many people have this problem, from a real source. Never invent a market size.

## Step 3, the honest scorecard

Rate each 1 to 5 with one line of evidence:
- How painful is the problem?
- How often does it happen?
- Can the user reach these people easily?
- Is there something the user knows or has that others don't?
- Could a small version be built in two weeks with Claude?

## Step 4, the cheapest test

Design one test that takes days, not months, and has a clear pass line decided in advance. Examples:
- Ten conversations with target users, with a script that asks about their last time having the problem (not "would you use my app").
- A one-page site with a signup button, shared in three group chats. Pass: 30 signups in a week.
- Doing the service by hand for five people before building anything.

Write the questions for user conversations. Good ones ask about past behavior ("tell me about the last time...") and never pitch.

## Step 5, verdict

One of: **build a small version**, **run the test first**, **change the idea** (and how), or **drop it**. Be direct. A kind "drop it" now saves the user a semester.

Save everything to `idea-check.md` so it can be updated after the test.
