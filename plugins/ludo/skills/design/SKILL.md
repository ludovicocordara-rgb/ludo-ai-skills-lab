# Good taste

Most AI-built pages look the same: a purple gradient, a centered headline over a dark blob, three glowing cards with icons, pill badges, huge numbers. People recognize it instantly and it reads as cheap. This skill steers toward pages that look edited, calm and expensive.

## Before designing

Ask, or infer from the request:
1. **Who is it for, and what should they do?** One primary action per page.
2. **What is the feeling?** Pick one: editorial (like a good magazine), technical (like good documentation), warm (like a small studio), bold (like a poster). Commit to it for the whole page.
3. **What is the real content?** Design around the actual words and images. Never design around lorem ipsum.

## Defaults that almost always work

**Type**
- Two typefaces at most: one for headings, one for body. A serif heading with a clean sans body is hard to get wrong.
- Restrained sizes. Hero headline around 56 to 76px on desktop, section heads around 36 to 42px, body 16 to 18px. Giant type reads as childish.
- Sentence case everywhere. No all-caps labels stacked above headings.
- Line length around 60 to 75 characters for reading text.

**Color**
- A near-white or warm off-white background, a near-black text color, and one accent color used sparingly (links, one button, one highlight).
- No gradients on text or buttons. No glow.

**Layout**
- Generous whitespace. When in doubt, add space, remove a box.
- Full-width sections with clean hard edges. Avoid boxes inside boxes.
- Thin 1px rules to separate things instead of cards with shadows.
- One visual language across the page. A single dark panel in a light page looks like a mistake.

**Content**
- Less text. A headline and one sentence per section; details can live one click or hover away.
- Show the real thing working (a screenshot, a live chart, an example output) instead of describing it.
- Numbers at reading size inside sentences, not blown up to 96px.

**Motion**
- Subtle and smooth. Fade or slide in place; never make content vanish and redraw.
- Animate charts only when they scroll into view.

Common page layouts that work:
- **Personal site:** name and one line on top, three to five projects as simple rows (title, one line, link), a short about, contact at the bottom.
- **Landing page:** headline and one line, one screenshot of the real product, three short "how it works" steps, one call to action repeated at the end.
- **Dashboard:** the three numbers that matter at the top in plain rows, one main chart, a table below. Filters in one line above the table.
- **Portfolio or case study:** big image, what the problem was, what you did, what changed (with a number), one lesson.

## Banned by default

Purple-to-blue gradients, glassmorphism everywhere, three equal feature cards with icons, pill badges on every heading, heavy drop shadows, stock "diverse team laughing" photos, emoji as icons, "Trusted by" logo strips with made-up logos, fake testimonials, and invented statistics.

## Check before showing it

Open it in a browser at desktop width and at phone width (about 390px). Look for: text touching edges, overlapping elements, horizontal scrolling on phone, anything that looks crowded. Fix those before calling it done.
