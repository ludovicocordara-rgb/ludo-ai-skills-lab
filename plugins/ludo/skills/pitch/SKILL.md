# Pitch

Good copy is specific, short, and about the reader. Bad copy is about the writer and full of adjectives. This skill writes the first kind.

## Step 1, three questions first

Ask, multiple choice where possible:
1. **Who exactly is reading?** Not "students". "First-years who think coding isn't for them."
2. **What is the one action?** Sign up, apply, show up, buy, reply.
3. **Why would they bother?** The single strongest reason, in their words. If there's proof (a number, a result, a name people trust), get it now.

## Step 2, write

**Structure for a page or post:**
1. **Headline**: the outcome for the reader, in plain words. Under 10 words.
2. **One line** that makes it concrete: what it is, for whom, and what it costs them (time, money).
3. **Proof**: a real example, number, quote or result. Only true ones.
4. **What happens next**: exactly what they get or do, step by step, so it feels easy.
5. **The action**: one button or one instruction, repeated at the end.

**Rules**
- Specific beats clever. "Build a working app in 90 minutes" beats "Unlock your potential".
- Talk to one person: "you", not "users".
- Short sentences. Cut every word that doesn't earn its place.
- No hype words: revolutionary, seamless, cutting-edge, game-changing, elevate, empower.
- No fake urgency and no invented numbers, testimonials or logos.

## Step 3, versions

Give three headline options with one line each on the angle they take (outcome, curiosity, proof). Recommend one.

For social posts: a version for LinkedIn (a short story, line breaks, no hashtag spam), one for Instagram or a group chat (two lines and the link).

## Step 4, check

Read it as the target reader. Can they tell in five seconds what it is and what to do? If not, cut until they can.
